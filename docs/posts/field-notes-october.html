Field Notes, October

12 October 2026 · 1 min

The light changes first. It comes in lower now, at an angle that makes every wall look newly built, and the shadows last all afternoon instead of passing through like a rumor.

A few things I wrote down this month:

  • The sound of a street being swept before anyone is awake.
  • A café that plays one radio station, quietly, and has done so for years.
  • The exact moment a screen goes from blue to warm, just before sunset.
  • A woman on the train reading a paper map, tracing the route with a fingernail.
  • The word meanwhile, which is doing a lot of quiet work in most sentences.

None of it belongs anywhere. That is the point of a field note: it is not an argument, only evidence.

I have been trying to pay attention without immediately turning the attention into something else—a post, a photograph, a lesson. Some days it works. Most days I reach for the camera, and the moment folds itself away politely, the way a cat leaves a room when you finally decide to look at it.

Tomorrow: the same walk, slower.
